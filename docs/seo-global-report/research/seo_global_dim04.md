## 7. WHO Suicide Prevention Guidelines for Digital Content

### 7.1 WHO Content Guidelines (CRITICAL) [^176^] [^66^]

The World Health Organization provides specific guidelines for digital suicide prevention content:

#### Search Engine Recommendations [^66^]:
> "When suicide-related keywords are used in searches on the web, it is recommended that **crisis helpline information, websites for suicide prevention organizations, and survival guides be displayed prominently** on the pages of search results."

> "When suicide-related keywords are typed into the search box, it is recommended that **no related search terms, especially those referring to self-harm or suicide, be prompted** to help formulate further searches."

#### Social Media Recommendations [^66^]:
- Suggest referrals to professionals from suicide prevention organizations
- Forums should **avoid discussions on suicide methods, locations, or forming suicide pacts**
- Use hashtags to encourage help-seeking
- Beware of cyberbullying
- Provide users with general guidelines on proper use

### 7.2 WHO LIVE LIFE Strategy [^330^]

WHO's official implementation guide specifies four key evidence-based interventions:

| Intervention | Description | Digital Content Application |
|--------------|-------------|---------------------------|
| **L**imit access to means | Restrict lethal means | Content about safe storage, lethal means counseling |
| **I**nteract with media | Responsible reporting | Content creator guidelines, media partnerships |
| **F**oster life skills | Socio-emotional skills in adolescents | School resources, youth-focused content |
| **E**arly identify | Assess, manage, follow up | Screening tools, risk assessment education |

### 7.3 WHO Four Dimensions of Responsible Coverage [^176^]

| Dimension | "Do" | "Don't" |
|-----------|------|---------|
| **Prominence** | Include prevention info prominently | Don't put suicide in headline |
| **Complexity** | Explain multiple contributing factors | Don't attribute to single cause |
| **Sensationalism** | Provide factual, calm presentation | Don't romanticize or glorify |
| **Prevention** | Include risk factors, help resources | Don't describe methods or locations |

### 7.4 WHO Safe Language Guidelines [^178^] [^301^]

| Avoid Using | Use Instead |
|-------------|-------------|
| "Committed suicide" | "Died by suicide" |
| "Successful suicide" | "Death by suicide" |
| "Failed suicide attempt" | "Survived a suicide attempt" |
| "Suicide victim" | "Person who died by suicide" |

## 14. YMYL Content Quality Standards

### 14.1 What is YMYL? [^233^] [^234^] [^235^] [^236^]

**"Your Money or Your Life"** content is defined by Google as content that could significantly impact:
- Health and well-being
- Financial stability
- Personal safety
- Major life decisions

**Suicide prevention content is among the highest-stakes YMYL categories.**

### 14.2 Google's Stricter Standards for YMYL [^234^] [^236^]

> "Where our algorithms detect that a user's query relates to a 'YMYL' topic, we will give more weight in our ranking systems to factors like our understanding of the **authoritativeness, expertise, or trustworthiness** of the pages we present in response." — Google, 2019 White Paper [^234^]

Google's approach:
1. YMYL content rated using **stricter standards** by Search Quality Raters
2. Failing pages receive **lowest quality rating**
3. Low-rated pages are **"untrustworthy, deceptive, harmful"** [^236^]
4. Ratings feed into algorithm updates
5. Low-quality YMYL pages eventually **removed from SERPs**

### 14.3 E-E-A-T for Suicide Prevention Content [^233^] [^237^]

| **Expertise** | Medical/mental health credentials; clinical background |
| **Authoritativeness** | Recognition by NIMH, SAMHSA, WHO, AFSP; citations from .edu/.gov |
| **Trustworthiness** | Accuracy verified by experts; transparent authorship; crisis info current |

## 17. Social Media Crisis Prevention Content Strategies

### 17.1 Social Media Guidelines for Suicide Content [^66^] [^178^] [^179^]

#### Platform-Specific Recommendations [^66^]:

- **Include suggestive referrals** to suicide prevention professionals in responses
- **Avoid discussions on suicide methods, locations, or forming pacts**
- **Use hashtags** to encourage help-seeking
- **Beware of cyberbullying** — victims are in high-risk groups
- **Provide guidelines** on proper use, depression awareness, distress responses

#### Content Warning Best Practices [^178^]:

> "Viewer Discretion may be advised: This post discusses suicide. If you are in need of help or support, please contact a crisis hotline."

#### For Those Sharing Lived Experience [^178^]:
- Reflect before posting (why, what, who will see)
- Only disclose what you're comfortable sharing
- Provide content warnings for graphic/distressing content
- Add disclaimers — you're sharing experience, not giving expert advice
- Encourage help-seeking with resources
- Consider consulting a mental health professional

### 17.2 SANE Australia Campaign Data [^232^]

The "Better Off With You" campaign provides empirical social media insights:

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Video completion rate | Higher for male viewers with male storytellers |
| Peak engagement times | 2-3 AM and 8-9 PM |
| Mobile access | 67.9% of website visitors |
| Social media vs website | More people scroll than click through to website |
| Friday pattern | Highest information-seeking behavior |
| Escalation risk | Low — people unlikely to post vulnerable comments publicly |

### 17.3 Social Media Platform Considerations [^229^]

Research on TikTok's mental health recommendations found:
- Content classified as **"Potentially Supportive"** (professional advice, evidence-based coping) vs. **"Potentially Harmful"** (normalizes harmful behaviors, misinformation)
- Critical safety indicators: explicit suicidal ideation, self-harm depiction, crisis helpline information
- Algorithm-driven recommendations may inadvertently amplify harmful content [^229^]

### 17.4 Real-Time Suicide Surveillance (RTSS) [^234^]

Public health researchers recommend:
- Integrating **social media monitoring** into RTSS systems
- Tracking suicide-related discourse and digital exposure in real-time
- Developing targeted interventions to curb toxic discourse and misinformation
- Actively promoting protective content directing users to reliable resources

## 20. Postvention Content Ranking Considerations

### 20.1 What is Postvention? [^293^] [^294^]

Postvention involves **"activities related to the help and support of people bereaved after suicide."** [^294^]

### 20.2 Postvention Service Levels [^294^]

| Level | Target | Services |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Universal** | Low impact, normal grief | Information: leaflets, booklets, factsheets, online info |
| **Selective** | Moderate grief | Support services, support groups, self-help, helplines |
| **Indicated** | Severe grief | Counseling, one-to-one psychological help |
| **Clinical** | Complicated grief/mental health | Psychotherapy, evidence-based treatments |

### 20.3 Postvention Content SEO Strategy

| Content Type | Keywords | YMYL Level |
|--------------|----------|------------|
| Grief after suicide | "suicide loss", "grief after suicide", "suicide bereavement" | High |
| Supporting the bereaved | "how to help suicide survivor", "supporting someone after suicide" | High |
| Coping with loss | "coping with suicide loss", "suicide survivor support" | High |
| Workplace postvention | "suicide in the workplace", "employee suicide response" | High |
| School postvention | "student suicide response", "school suicide crisis" | Extreme |

### 20.4 Postvention Content Guidelines

Based on Postvention Australia [^294^] and US National Guidelines [^293^]:

1. **Use public health model framework** — universal, selective, indicated strategies
2. **Address the continuum of survivorship** — from close family to community members
3. **Include practical tools** — checklists, templates, scripts
4. **Acknowledge workforce impact** — compassion fatigue, vicarious trauma
5. **Promote self-care** for those supporting the bereaved
6. **Include cultural considerations** — diverse grief responses
7. **Provide referral pathways** — always link to professional support

### 20.5 Sample Postvention Content Structure

```
H1: Supporting Someone Bereaved by Suicide
H2: Understanding Suicide Loss
H2: What to Say to Someone Who Lost a Loved One to Suicide
H2: What NOT to Say
H2: Practical Ways to Help
H2: When to Encourage Professional Support
H2: Resources for Suicide Loss Survivors
  H3: Support Groups
  H3: Counseling Services
  H3: Crisis Lines (988)
H2: Taking Care of Yourself as a Supporter
```
