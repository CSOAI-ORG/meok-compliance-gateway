## 5. Food, Tech, Mobile & Tools Cluster

### 5.1 Cluster Overview and Market Opportunity

#### 5.1.1 Seven Domains Spanning Five Interconnected Verticals

The Food, Tech, Mobile & Tools cluster encompasses seven domains: AI-powered meal planning (**meok.ai**), food delivery (**openmoe.ai**), mobile optimization (**optimobile.ai**), home improvement and DIY guidance (**diyhelp.ai**), factory automation (**loopfactory.ai**), and AI security (**asisecurity.ai**, **agisafe.ai**). The unifying thread is that all seven domains sit at the intersection of artificial intelligence and established industries undergoing digital transformation, creating both outsized SEO opportunity and significant competitive complexity.

#### 5.1.2 Market Sizes and Growth Trajectories

The AI meal planning market was valued at $972.1 million in 2024 and is projected to reach $11.57 billion by 2034, representing a 28.1% CAGR. [^240^] A parallel estimate places the AI-generated meal plan market at $1.34 billion in 2025, growing to $10.77 billion by 2035. [^233^] The recipe apps market is projected to reach $15.14 billion by 2035 at a 9.57% CAGR, with North America holding 32.1% of global share. [^237^] [^240^]

Food delivery represents a larger but more mature opportunity: the global online market was valued at $319.99 billion in 2025, projected to reach $728.83 billion by 2034 at a 9.58% CAGR. [^45^] Mobile ordering dominates, with 55.8% of revenue flowing through mobile applications and 95% of DoorDash orders placed on mobile devices. [^44^] [^344^] This mobile-first reality makes App Store Optimization (ASO) inseparable from web SEO for any food delivery entrant.

The DIY and home improvement market was valued at $861.4 billion globally in 2024, growing at a 5.0% CAGR. [^60^] The U.S. home services segment reached $520 billion in 2023 and is projected to approach $1.03 trillion by 2030 at a 10.5% CAGR. [^373^] Factory automation sits at a $141.5 billion baseline (2024) with projections reaching $353 billion by 2034 at a 9.4% CAGR, while the digital twin market — a keyword-rich sub-segment — is expected to grow from $21.14 billion to $149.81 billion by 2030 at a 47.9% CAGR. [^150^] [^366^]

The AI cybersecurity market was valued at approximately $19.2 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $64.5 billion by 2030 at a 22.8% CAGR. [^66^] Within this field, AI red teaming represents a $4.2 billion market growing to $22.8 billion by 2034 at a 20.5% CAGR, while adversarial machine learning constitutes a $1.64 billion segment projected to reach $5.67 billion by 2030 at a 28.3% CAGR. [^2^] [^3^]

#### 5.1.3 AI Cybersecurity: An Emerging Institutional Priority

Beyond commercial market sizing, the AI security vertical benefits from rapidly accelerating government funding that creates organic search demand around policy and technical keywords. The UK AI Security Institute (formerly AISI) received £100 million in funding until 2030, while the U.S. AI Safety Institute at NIST received $10 million for fiscal year 2024/25. [^332^] [^342^] Open Philanthropy issued a $40 million request for proposals for technical AI safety research in 2026, and Canada committed CA$50 million to its national AI Safety Institute over five years. [^301^] [^334^] The International Network of AI Safety Institutes launched in November 2024 with ten initial member countries, signaling institutional momentum that will drive search volume for governance-related keywords. [^337^] [^338^] This funding trajectory suggests that AI security and safety keywords will experience organic demand growth regardless of commercial content investment — a rare combination of market-driven and institutionally driven search expansion.

### 5.3 Competitive Landscape

#### 5.3.1 Food AI: Mealime, PlateJoy, and Eat This Much

The AI meal planning space is crowded with well-funded incumbents. Mealime commands 4 million+ users with a freemium model targeting "simple, healthy meal planning." [^155^] PlateJoy competes through healthcare partnerships, while Eat This Much owns the fitness community with calorie-specific content clusters. [^233^] [^234^] The content gap meok.ai can exploit lies at the intersection of AI personalization and dietary specificity — queries such as "how does AI meal planning work," "AI meal planner vs nutritionist," and "best AI meal planner for [specific diet]" remain underserved. Grocery integration content represents a secondary gap: grocery-syncing functionality drives retention but receives limited SEO attention from current players. [^237^]

#### 5.3.2 Food Delivery: DoorDash, UberEats, and Ghost Kitchen SEO

DoorDash dominates food delivery SEO by optimizing aggressively for "near me" local search at scale — 95% of its orders come from mobile. [^344^] UberEats leverages the broader Uber brand ecosystem for cross-app discovery. [^42^] The search term "restaurants near me" generates 6.2 million organic searches monthly, while "food near me open now" has increased 99% year-over-year. [^305^] Google's local ranking framework evaluates Relevance, Distance, and Prominence, meaning a new entrant cannot outrank established platforms on proximity alone. [^239^] However, 76% of nearby mobile searchers visit a related business within a day. [^293^] For openmoe.ai, the competitive wedge lies in ghost kitchen and virtual restaurant SEO — segments where incumbents have limited specialist content.

#### 5.3.3 DIY: Family Handyman, Bob Vila, and DIY.com

The DIY content space is dominated by Family Handyman (extensive how-to library with seasonal publishing cycles), Bob Vila (decades of brand authority and media backlinks), and DIY.com (e-commerce-plus-content hybrid with product-focused SEO). [^326^] Their primary differentiator is update frequency: Family Handyman publishes weekly, Bob Vila focuses on fewer expert-driven pieces, and DIY.com refreshes daily around product inventory. For diyhelp.ai to compete, the strategy must introduce a format these incumbents do not offer — AI-assisted interactive content such as cost calculators and material estimators that generate unique page experiences, drive return visits, and create engagement signals that static publishing models cannot replicate.

#### 5.3.4 Factory Automation: Siemens, Rockwell, and Schneider Electric

The factory automation SEO landscape is defined by industrial conglomerates using thought leadership as a top-of-funnel mechanism. Siemens ranks for "Industry 4.0" and "digital twin" through its Xcelerator platform. Rockwell Automation targets process verticals with white papers and webinars. Schneider Electric — which announced a €2 billion AI investment in November 2025 — has built authority around energy-efficient manufacturing. [^266^] The competitive gap for loopfactory.ai lies in the sustainability-meets-automation intersection: keywords such as "sustainable manufacturing" (10K-20K monthly searches, Low-Medium difficulty) and "carbon neutral factory" (1K-3K, emerging) align with ESG-driven procurement trends but lack authoritative content clusters from established players. [^366^] [^374^]

### 5.4 The ASI/AGI Keyword Arbitrage

#### 5.4.1 "ASI Security": Zero Competition with Institutional Tailwinds

The term "ASI security" — Artificial Superintelligence security — represents the most extreme keyword arbitrage opportunity identified across all 25 portfolio domains. Keyword difficulty estimates range from 0 to 5, with virtually zero commercial competition. [^289^] No existing website systematically targets "ASI security" or "artificial superintelligence cybersecurity" as primary keywords. The concept is used explicitly by the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI) and referenced in academic safety literature, but the search namespace remains entirely unclaimed by commercial entities. [^285^] [^289^]

This absence of competition would typically signal insufficient search demand. However, three structural factors indicate rapidly expanding interest. First, the UK AI Security Institute received £100 million through 2030, creating institutional visibility for ASI terminology. [^332^] Second, AI alignment research citations have grown 300%+ since 2019, with sub-fields such as mechanistic interpretability growing 800%+. [^265^] [^267^] Third, the U.S. Department of Defense published AI/ML supply chain risk guidance in March 2026, signaling military-level institutional attention. [^210^]

The arbitrage strategy is namespace ownership — not lead generation. asisecurity.ai should publish the definitive reference content ("What is ASI Security?", "ASI Cybersecurity Frameworks", "ASI Threat Landscape 2026") that becomes the default citation for subsequent ASI discourse. The goal is to become the reference that LLMs, researchers, and policymakers cite — a positioning that compounds over time.

#### 5.4.2 "AGI Safety" Academic Keywords: Low Competition, High Citation Velocity

While "ASI security" occupies the extreme low-competition end of the spectrum, "AGI safety" and its semantic variants present a slightly more developed but still highly accessible keyword landscape. Keyword difficulty for "AGI safety" ranges from 15 to 25, with "artificial general intelligence safety" at 10-15 and "AGI safety research" at 12-18. [^278^] [^284^] [^285^] These difficulty levels place these terms firmly in the "achievable for a new domain with focused authority building" range, particularly given the niche nature of the content required.

The academic citation velocity in this space is exceptional. AI alignment research has grown over 300% since 2019, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) citations have grown over 1,000%, and constitutional AI citations have grown over 500%. [^265^] [^267^] [^268^] This citation growth translates directly into search demand as researchers, journalists, policymakers, and technologists seek to understand emerging safety concepts. The top-ranking organizations for AGI safety keywords — Anthropic (DA 85+), MIRI (DA 65+), the Alignment Forum (DA 60+), and the Future of Life Institute (DA 70+) — are non-commercial research organizations that do not optimize for search in the traditional sense. [^277^] [^278^] [^284^] [^285^] [^289^] This creates a window where a commercially operated domain with genuine technical depth can rank alongside these institutions by meeting standards of academic rigor while providing superior page experience and content accessibility.

#### 5.4.3 Strategy: Own Emerging Vocabulary Before Competition

The ASI/AGI keyword arbitrage follows a predictable pattern observed across emerging technology domains: terminology that begins in academic or research contexts migrates into policy discourse, then into media coverage, then into commercial procurement and vendor evaluation. Each stage of migration produces a new wave of search demand. By publishing authoritative content at the earliest stage — academic terminology definition and research synthesis — asisecurity.ai and agisafe.ai establish credibility that persists as the keyword landscape matures and commercial competition intensifies.

The content investment profile for this strategy differs fundamentally from commercial SEO. Rather than optimizing for conversion rate or lead generation, the primary metrics are citation rate (by academic and policy publications), backlink acquisition from .edu and .gov domains, and LLM citation frequency. The recommended content mix is 70% thought leadership (research summaries, framework papers, policy analysis) and 30% educational (explainer content, glossaries, career resources). This ratio inverts the typical commercial SEO mix and reflects the reality that AGI safety searchers are primarily researchers, policymakers, and technologists rather than commercial buyers.

### 5.5 ASO + SEO + GEO Triple Optimization

#### 5.5.1 The App Store as a Search Engine

App Store Optimization (ASO) is not a supplementary channel to web SEO — for the three domains in this cluster with app components (meok.ai, openmoe.ai, optimobile.ai), it is the primary user acquisition channel. Seventy percent of App Store users discover apps via search, and over 60% of all app downloads originate directly from store search. [^188^] [^274^] The top three positions in app store search results capture approximately 50% of all downloads for a given query, while rankings beyond the tenth position receive negligible traffic. [^188^] Updating store visuals alone — screenshots, preview videos, and iconography — can increase install rates by 20-50%. [^274^]

The ASO tools market, valued at $1.5 billion in 2025 and projected to reach $4.5 billion by 2034 at a 13.5% CAGR, reflects the increasing sophistication of app store search as a competitive channel. [^61^] Both Apple and Google have made algorithmic shifts that reinforce the SEO-ASO convergence: Apple now indexes screenshot captions for keyword ranking (mid-2025 change), and both platforms have shifted ranking weight from download counts toward retention and engagement metrics. [^28^] These changes mean that app content quality — measured through session duration, return visits, and in-app engagement — directly impacts search visibility, mirroring the behavioral signals that Google's web search algorithm has incorporated for years.

#### 5.5.2 Unified Content Strategy: Single Source, Triple Distribution

The convergence of ASO, SEO, and GEO creates an architectural requirement: web content, app content, and AI-optimized summaries must originate from a single source of truth. Inconsistencies across channels degrade trust signals, while Google's Firebase App Indexing now surfaces app content directly in search results and AI Overviews can cite app-based answers. [^188^] [^274^]

The unified architecture requires three components: (1) a headless CMS distributing structured data to web, app, and AI endpoints simultaneously; (2) schema markup for cross-channel entity recognition — Recipe, SoftwareApplication, and HowTo schema as appropriate; (3) a keyword mapping system tracking topics across web search, app store search, and generative search to identify intent divergence.
